SKYLINE / PROJECT KNOWLEDGE CENTRE

Plan carefully. Build with clarity.

A step-by-step reference for homeowners, investors and project teams planning construction in Islamabad and Rawalpindi. Use it to organise questions, documents, budgets and site decisions before work begins.

DISCUSS PROJECT PLANNING ↗

Construction is a sequence of connected decisions.

Plot status, design, authority requirements, soil conditions, structural design, procurement, workmanship and cash flow affect one another. Treat this guide as a planning checklist and confirm legal, technical and approval matters with the relevant authority and qualified professionals.

PHASE 01 / DUE DILIGENCE

Verify plot, title and approvals

  • Confirm exact plot number, block, sector, phase and dimensions from original documents.
  • Verify ownership/title and encumbrances through appropriate official channels and legal advice.
  • Check the relevant authority and current scheme status, layout approval and NOC separately where applicable.
  • Obtain current building-control regulations, setbacks, height, coverage and parking requirements.
  • Check access, road level, easements, utilities, drainage and any site restrictions.

A marketing brochure or an approved layout plan alone does not prove every required approval or title condition.

PHASE 02 / DESIGN

Prepare coordinated drawings

  • Brief the architect on rooms, occupancy, orientation, future needs and budget.
  • Commission soil investigation where required by the engineer and site conditions.
  • Obtain structural design and drawings from a qualified structural engineer.
  • Coordinate architectural, structural, plumbing, electrical and HVAC layouts.
  • Resolve dimensions, levels, openings, staircases, shafts and service routes before construction.
  • Maintain revision numbers and a clear record of approved drawings.
PHASE 03 / COST CONTROL

Prepare a BOQ and realistic budget

A bill of quantities turns a broad idea into measurable work packages.

  • Separate excavation, foundations, concrete, reinforcement, formwork and masonry.
  • List plaster, waterproofing, flooring, paint, doors/windows and external works.
  • Separate plumbing, electrical, fixtures, equipment and specialist systems.
  • State units, quantities, specifications, inclusions and exclusions.
  • Include design/professional fees, approvals, utility connections, taxes and contingency as applicable.
  • Compare like-for-like quotations, not just headline per-square-foot rates.
PHASE 04 / PROCUREMENT

Plan sequence, cash flow and deliveries

  • Create a milestone programme with dependencies and realistic durations.
  • Identify long-lead items such as windows, lifts, specialist equipment and custom finishes.
  • Order materials against approved specifications and a delivery schedule.
  • Check site storage, security, unloading access and protection from weather.
  • Track committed cost, paid amounts, pending invoices and forecast cash flow.
  • Record every variation before it is carried out.
PHASE 05 / QUALITY

Inspect work at the right stages

  • Use approved drawings, method statements and inspection checklists.
  • Check reinforcement size, spacing, cover, laps and embedded services before concrete pours.
  • Verify formwork, levels, cleanliness, concrete delivery and curing procedures.
  • Inspect masonry alignment, openings, mortar and service chases.
  • Test waterproofing before covering it with screed or finishes.
  • Keep material submittals, delivery records, test reports and inspection approvals.

Inspection criteria and testing frequency must come from the project specification and responsible engineer.

PHASE 06 / SITE SAFETY

Protect workers, visitors and neighbours

  • Set site access rules, emergency contacts and visitor controls.
  • Use appropriate PPE and task-specific risk assessments.
  • Provide safe scaffolds, edge protection, ladders and excavation controls.
  • Manage electrical temporary supplies and isolate unsafe equipment.
  • Control dust, noise, debris, lifting operations and traffic interfaces.
  • Keep first-aid and emergency arrangements visible and maintained.
PHASE 07 / CLOSE-OUT

Complete checks before handover

  • Prepare a snag list room by room and close each item with evidence.
  • Test plumbing, drainage, electrical protection and installed equipment.
  • Collect warranties, manuals, test certificates and approved product details.
  • Record final drawings and any approved changes from the original design.
  • Agree keys, access devices, maintenance guidance and outstanding items.
  • Keep final accounts, receipts and signed completion records.
ONGOING / DOCUMENT CONTROL

Keep one reliable project record

Maintain a dated record of site instructions, meeting notes, drawing revisions, approvals, inspection results, delivery challans, invoices, variations, photographs and payment certificates. Clear records help resolve questions before they become disputes.

BUDGETING BASICS

What a per-square-foot estimate can and cannot tell you.

It can help with early planning

A rate applied to a clearly defined covered area can provide a preliminary comparison when the scope and specification are consistent.

It cannot replace a BOQ

Rates vary with structure, soil, access, basement, spans, floor heights, material brands, finish schedule, MEP, external works and market conditions. Confirm inclusions and exclusions.

Frequently asked questions

Should I start construction before the approved drawings are ready?

Do not proceed with work that requires approvals or design information before those are in place. Confirm the authority requirements and have the responsible professionals issue the necessary drawings and instructions.

Is the cheapest quotation always the best?

No. Compare identical scope, specifications, quantities, taxes, delivery, exclusions, programme, payment terms and quality controls. A low headline rate may omit important work.

Why should I keep a contingency?

Unforeseen ground conditions, design coordination, price changes and scope clarifications can affect the budget. Set a contingency appropriate to the project's risk with your professional adviser.

Can this guide confirm CDA or private-scheme permission?

No. Regulations and scheme status can change and depend on the exact plot. Check the latest official records and obtain written confirmation from the competent authority.

This guide is general educational information, not legal, architectural, structural, quantity-surveying or safety advice. Obtain project-specific advice from qualified professionals and the competent authority.
