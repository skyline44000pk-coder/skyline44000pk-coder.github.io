Practical site guidance

Before you buy materials or start building

Short checks that help reduce avoidable delays, disputes and rework.

01 / APPROVALS

Verify the plot and scheme first

Match the exact scheme, block, plot number and phase against the relevant authority's current records. Ask for the approved layout plan and current NOC/status; do not rely only on a marketing brochure or a seller's screenshot.

02 / STRUCTURE

Check steel before unloading

Confirm mill/brand marks, grade, nominal diameter and bar count against the structural engineer's schedule and purchase order. Record the delivered weight and reject bars with serious pitting, damage or mismatched specification.

03 / CEMENT

Store cement dry and off the floor

Check the manufacturing date, intact bags and batch information. Keep bags on a raised dry platform, away from walls and rain; use older stock first and do not use hardened or moisture-damaged bags.

04 / BRICKWORK

Agree brick quality and delivery terms

Inspect a sample for consistent dimensions, shape and visible cracks. Put the brick class, quantity, loading, breakage allowance and delivered-to-site price in writing before placing a bulk order.

05 / CONCRETE

Do not guess the concrete mix

Use the mix design and strength specified by the structural engineer. Confirm aggregate grading, water quality, batching, compaction and curing on site; a generic cement-to-sand ratio is not a substitute for a designed structural mix.

06 / BUDGET

Build a BOQ before fixing the budget

Separate structure, masonry, plaster, waterproofing, electrical, plumbing, doors/windows, finishes, external works and professional/approval costs. Keep a contingency and update rates using written local quotes.

Frequently asked questions

Common planning questions

Is there one official A+ construction rate per square foot?

No single official fixed A+ rate is stated here. Specification, structural system, soil, covered area, number of floors, finish brands, MEP scope and site access all affect the price. Enter a current written contractor rate in the calculator.

Why do 5 marla or 10 marla plots have different square-foot areas?

Marla conventions vary. This calculator lets you select 225 or 272.25 sq ft per marla, or use exact frontage and depth. The allotment letter and approved site plan should take priority over a generic marla label.

Can I use the CDA covered-area estimate in Bahria or DHA?

No. The CDA Schedule-1 estimate is limited to the selected CDA residential-sector category. Other authorities or schemes may use different rules. Confirm the current building-control authority and approved plan for the exact plot.

Does an approved layout plan mean a housing scheme has an NOC?

Not necessarily. CDA explains that private schemes in ICT have a two-step process: layout-plan approval and, after subsequent formalities, an NOC for development. Check both statuses and any later cancellation or extension notice.

Are the rates on this page guaranteed supplier prices?

No. They are dated published market indicators, not a supplier invoice or SKYLINE quotation. Request a written quote with exact brand, grade, size, quantity, tax and delivery terms before making a purchase.
