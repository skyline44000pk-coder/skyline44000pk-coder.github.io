01 · Before you pay

Property due diligence: check first, pay later.

Use this starter checklist before booking, token, advance or transfer. A brochure, dealer promise or social-media post is not proof of legal title or development approval.

OWNERSHIP

Confirm who can legally sell

Ask for the original allotment/transfer documents, seller identity and authority to transact. Match names, plot number, size, sector/block and property description across records. For inherited, jointly owned or company-owned property, check who must sign.

SITE & POSSESSION

Visit and verify the actual plot

Physically locate the plot with the society/authority record. Check possession, access road, boundaries, encroachment, utilities, nearby drains and any visible dispute. Do not rely only on a map pin or dealer's verbal location.

APPROVAL STATUS

Check the scheme and layout

Identify the competent authority for the exact location. Check the scheme's current approval/NOC status and whether the specific block, extension and plot are included in the approved layout. Approval of a scheme name does not automatically validate every file or extension.

Important: For a high-value transaction, obtain an independent property lawyer's title and encumbrance review and confirm current dues directly with the relevant authority or society. This guide is educational, not a title certificate or legal opinion.
02 · Paperwork

Documents buyers and sellers should understand.

Names and procedures vary by property type, province, authority and society. Ask the relevant office which current certified records apply to your case.

  • CNICs of buyer and seller; verify identity in person
  • Original allotment letter, transfer letter or title deed as applicable
  • Latest ownership/record extract from the competent land authority
  • Approved layout plan and plot demarcation details
  • Possession letter and site verification, where applicable
  • Latest society/authority dues, taxes and utility status
  • Mortgage, lien, stay order, litigation and encumbrance checks
  • Registered sale deed, mutation/record update and transfer receipt as applicable
  • For a house: approved building plan, completion/occupancy record where required
03 · Official verification desk

Check a housing society before you invest.

Use the regulator's own live records. Search the exact scheme name, location, phase and extension. A society's marketing name may differ from its registered name, so confirm the matching land and approved layout too.

ISLAMABAD CAPITAL TERRITORY

CDA official records

Check the private housing-scheme register for the Layout Plan (LOP) and NOC status. Then check CDA's separate illegal-schemes notice. CDA's register distinguishes approved LOP, provisional/cancelled LOP and NOC issued/not issued/cancelled.

OTHER AUTHORITIES

Not every scheme is regulated by CDA/RDA

For a society outside their jurisdiction, identify the competent regulator first. Punjab schemes may involve PHATA or a local development authority; cantonment, DHA and other areas can have different competent authorities. Ask the relevant regulator for written confirmation.

Punjab PHATA ↗

How to read the result: “LOP approved” is not the same as “NOC issued”. “NOC issued” is not a guarantee that every block, extension, file or individual plot is covered. Confirm the exact approved boundary/layout, phase, block, plot number, ownership, transfer status, dues and any court/authority notices before paying.

03 · Know the authority

There is no single approval office for all of Pakistan.

Start by identifying the city, district, land type, scheme and zone. The authority responsible for planning, building control, land record and utility connections may be different.

ISLAMABAD CAPITAL TERRITORY

CDA and ICT context

Check the exact sector/zone and whether CDA, an applicable private-scheme framework or another competent office handles the case. Use CDA's official laws, housing-scheme and public-notice registers for current information.

CDA laws & regulations ↗

RAWALPINDI / PUNJAB

RDA, local government and land records

Depending on location and development, check RDA, the relevant local government/development authority and Punjab land-record channels. Confirm the exact jurisdiction rather than assuming every Rawalpindi address is treated alike.

OTHER CITIES & SOCIETIES

Verify the local regulator

For Lahore, Karachi, Peshawar, Faisalabad and other cities, confirm the relevant development authority, municipal body, revenue office and society rules. DHA, Bahria and other schemes have their own processes, but private rules do not replace statutory approvals.

Never infer approval from marketing: Ask the regulator for written, current status of the exact scheme, phase, block and plot. An application under process, a public notice, an approved layout and a final NOC are different things.
04 · From idea to keys

Understand the main stages of building a house.

Actual sequence and design depend on the site, soil, approved drawings, structure, climate and local rules. A qualified architect and structural engineer should prepare and approve the technical design.

Brief & feasibility

Define plot, budget, family needs, room count, orientation, access, soil and utility constraints.

Survey & design

Get measured survey, architectural drawings, structural design and coordinated MEP plans.

Permissions

Confirm applicable building rules, submit required plans and obtain written permissions before work.

BOQ & procurement

Prepare itemised quantities, specifications, brands, rates, wastage and delivery schedule.

Foundation

Follow soil investigation and structural drawings; inspect excavation, reinforcement and concrete before pours.

Structure & masonry

Check concrete, steel placement, curing, wall alignment, openings and engineer-approved changes.

MEP & finishes

Coordinate plumbing, electrical, waterproofing, plaster, flooring, joinery and paint in the correct sequence.

Testing & handover

Test systems, close defects, collect warranties/as-builts and confirm completion requirements.

05 · Quality control

Small checks can prevent expensive mistakes.

Never replace engineering judgement with a generic online tip. Use approved drawings, project specifications, manufacturer instructions and inspection records.

  • Use only the current approved drawings at the work site
  • Check steel grade, bar diameter, spacing, laps and cover against engineer drawings
  • Verify cement type, batch condition and storage away from moisture
  • Check aggregate cleanliness and concrete mix requirements
  • Keep concrete placement, testing and curing records as specified
  • Pressure-test plumbing and test electrical protection before concealment
  • Complete roof, wet-area and basement waterproofing tests before finishes
  • Use PPE, safe access, edge protection and controlled site storage

Common questions

Frequently asked questions.

Is an approved housing scheme the same as an approved plot?

No. Confirm the exact phase, block, extension, plot number, land ownership, layout inclusion, possession and any dues or restrictions with the competent authority and society.

Can I build immediately after buying a plot?

Not necessarily. First confirm transfer/possession, applicable building-control authority, approved building plan, setbacks, covered-area limits, height, parking, fire and utility requirements, and any society NOC.

Can an online calculator give my final construction cost?

No. It can provide an early planning estimate only. Final cost depends on drawings, covered area, soil/foundation, structure, specifications, labour, site access, taxes, wastage, market rates and project management.

Does SKYLINE show verified properties for sale or rent on this page?

This page is an educational guide, not a live verified listing inventory. Use the SKYLINE Properties page to submit a buying, selling or rental enquiry. Property details and availability must be independently verified before a transaction.

Does this guide replace an architect, engineer or lawyer?

No. It helps you understand questions to ask. Engage qualified local professionals for title checks, legal advice, structural design, permits, inspections and safety decisions.

Information is general educational guidance for Pakistan. Laws, procedures, fees, approval status and technical standards can change and vary by jurisdiction. Always verify with the relevant official authority and qualified professionals before acting.
